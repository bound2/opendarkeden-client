- Vampire legends of the Malay Peninsula
On the Malay Peninsula people have long believed in a vampire called the Penanggalan. The Penanggalan has no body; it floats about with its entrails dangling beneath its neck, waiting for a chance to suck the blood of small children, and it is said to be especially fond of the blood of women who have just given birth. People hang thorny branches on their doors and window frames so that the Penanggalan's stomach or intestines catch on the thorns and it cannot get into the house.
- Vampire legends of Japan
Vampire tales are rare in the East, but unusually, Japan has a vampire called the Rokurokubi. Some Rokurokubi have necks that stretch to about a fathom, while others have heads that come right off the body and fly about. The Rokurokubi whose head comes off and wanders resembles the Penanggalan of the Malay Peninsula, attacking people and craving their blood or flesh. This vampire lives deep in the mountains where few people go and looks like an ordinary human, but it can be recognized by a red vein around its neck. To destroy one, roll its body away while the head is off wandering, and the head will never find its way back.
