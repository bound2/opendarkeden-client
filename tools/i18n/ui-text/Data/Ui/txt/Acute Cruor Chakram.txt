A Chakram representing Surya, the sun god of ancient India. Surya is the deified sun; in the Vedic and Brahmana periods of ancient India he once held a rank as high as Vishnu and Shiva, regarded as the greatest gods of Hinduism, but his influence has waned markedly in modern Hinduism. Even so, Surya's image survives on many ancient relics, and a good number of temples once dedicated to him still stand. Surya drives away the darkness, wakes people from sleep and sets them to work, and as the eye of all the gods he watches over the deeds of every living thing in this world. In short, the symbol of the sun god Surya is one that makes people open their eyes in the morning and spend a lively day.
