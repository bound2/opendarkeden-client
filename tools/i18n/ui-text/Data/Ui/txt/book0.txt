Many scholars wanted to explain vampires through science rather than legend. Among the various scientific explanations, one study linked them to rabies. A Spanish neurologist, writing in a neurology journal, analyzed the traits of vampires alongside the symptoms of rabies patients and argued that the vampire most likely grew out of a misunderstanding of people suffering from rabies, pointing out a number of similar symptoms that could lead people to mistake a rabies victim for a vampire. That vampires are generally male fits the fact that men are seven times more likely than women to contract rabies, and biting other people is also a symptom often seen in rabies patients.
1. They hate garlic, mirrors and the like: rabies patients are extremely sensitive to stimuli. Faced with water, light, smells, mirrors and so on, they grimace or make harsh noises and bare their teeth.
2. They roam at night: rabies patients suffer from insomnia and tend to wander at night. Rabies also affects the part of the brain that controls emotion and behavior, producing excessive sexual desire.
3. They turn into bats or other animals: the rabies virus can infect animals such as bats, dogs and wolves and give them the symptoms of rabies, and people seem to have identified these animals with rabies patients.
4. Their corpses look alive: in someone who died of rabies the blood clots slowly, so blood may flow from the mouth. Moreover, in cold, damp regions such as the Balkan Peninsula of Eastern Europe, a corpse can be preserved for months or even years.
5. The origin of the vampire legend: records show that a great rabies epidemic swept Hungary between 1721 and 1728. This matches the time and place in which the vampire legend began.
