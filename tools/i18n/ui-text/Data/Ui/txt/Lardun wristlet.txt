A wristlet holding an Abraxas talisman of the kind used by the Gnostics. In the Greco-Roman world of the 2nd century, not long after Christianity arose, a doctrine called Gnosticism was widespread. Gnosticism stressed gnosis, a secret knowledge that cannot be grasped by reason and can only be gained in mystical ways such as divine revelation, and believed in a god greater and more almighty than the God of Christianity. The Gnostics were especially passionate about making talismans, and many were made in this period. They believed that carrying the Abraxas would drive away evil spirits and ward off misfortune.
