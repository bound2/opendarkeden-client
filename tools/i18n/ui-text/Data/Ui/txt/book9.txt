Vampires have several weaknesses. Everywhere it is agreed that a vampire comes out only at night and must return to its grave before the cock crows at dawn. First of all, they are vulnerable to sunlight. Exposed to it, a vampire's body burns up, and they cannot cross running water. Vampires also fear holy water - the source of life - the communion bread, and the cross. And one thing more: a vampire dies if silver, the holy metal, enters its body. The best way to put an end to this blasphemous being is to drive a stake through its heart. People of old said the only way to be rid of a vampire forever at a single stroke was to drive a wooden stake into its heart. The most common method is to drive the stake into its heart while it sleeps. But vampires are rarely found asleep; far more often they strike first. At such times it is almost impossible to stake a vampire through the heart. To harm a vampire, one must use magic or enchanted weapons. When struck with a silver weapon, even a small wound can kill a vampire. At the full moon vampires feel the craving for blood, and they are wounded by silver and magic weapons. The cross and holy water are also among the means of driving them off. The surest method of all is said to be exposing the vampire to sunlight.

Superstition holds that the only way to be rid of a vampire forever is to drive a wooden stake into its heart. The Russians used aspen, the wood of Christ's cross, while other countries, mindful of the crown of thorns, preferred hawthorn. On the Adriatic coast of Dalmatia and Albania the weapon was a dagger blessed beforehand by a priest. In Romania a rite called the 'great atonement' had to be performed at the break of dawn, and the executioner had to drive in the stake with a single blow so that the vampire could not rise again. If the corpse did not turn to ash, its head was cut off with a gravedigger's spade and the rest was burned, and the ashes were scattered to the wind or buried at a crossroads.
