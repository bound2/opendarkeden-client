The serpent-patterned symbol of the Raetra Chakram can be called both a labyrinth and a mandala. The chakram is modeled on a pattern that the Navajo, an Indian people of North America, used as a charm to heal illness. A mandala is originally a mystical picture made in India and Tibet as a tool for meditation, and its form recalls a labyrinth. The symbolic meaning of the labyrinth is that it surrounds the center, which represents one's true nature, and protects it from the approach of evil. The Navajo regarded the serpent as an immortal, mystical being with special healing energy. At the chakram's center is a grip divided into five spaces, symbolizing the unity of the elements that make up the human soul. The Navajo serpent mandala thus becomes a powerful symbol that guards and heals the human soul.
