Among the folk beliefs handed down among the Slavic peoples, the belief in vampires is a peculiar one.
The Slavic words meaning 'vampire' all derive from Qpyr, and the belief shares a common root. Among the Slavic peoples it was believed that sinners, sorcerers, werewolves and the like did not decay after death but became vampires, and that those who met their death by suicide or similar means became vampires as well. The corpse of one who has become a vampire has a red face and wide, staring eyes.
In Serbia it was believed that a vampire and a human woman could produce a deformed child with neither bones nor teeth, and that only this child could recognize a vampire and kill it. The Slavic belief that sinners become vampires comes from the idea that the earth, mother of all things, refuses to accept their bodies. Yet in the bitter cold of the Slavic north, the fact that buried corpses really did not decay was reason enough.
