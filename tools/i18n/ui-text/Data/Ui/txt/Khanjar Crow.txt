Khanjar means 'meat-cutting knife' in Arabic. It originated in Turkey; it is about 30 to 40cm long overall and weighs 0.3kg. The blade curves in an 's' shape, and some are finely gilded and engraved with patterns. Such khanjar knives were shrunk to about the size of a finger and used as the blades of this claw. The part that protects the hand, joined to the blades, is made from the bones and hide of a monster called the 'bunyip', giving it light weight and excellent shock absorption.
