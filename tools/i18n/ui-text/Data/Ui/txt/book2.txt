- Vampire legends of Romania
Romania has an unusually large number of vampire legends. An old folk tale says that "vampires swarmed like blades of grass in a meadow and sucked people's living blood every night." Long ago the people of this country feared vampires so much that even after burying the dead they would dig them up from time to time to see whether they had become vampires. Vampires here are also said to come in two kinds: those who become vampires while still alive, and those who rise as vampires from a corpse.
- Vampire legends of Czechoslovakia
In 1591 a shoemaker killed himself in a village in the region of Silesia. People there believed that suicides became vampires, so it was the custom to cut the body into pieces and burn it. But the shoemaker's wife insisted it had not been suicide, and the body was buried in the ground. Then the corpse came back to life and began appearing in the village, causing an uproar. When the alarmed villagers dug up the grave and opened the coffin, they found his heart still fresh and color still in his face.
