Among the tales of human monsters that have never died out since ancient times is that of people who change into animals. Apart from the werewolf, the animal naturally varies from region to region, but most reported werewolf sightings came from Europe. History holds an enormous number of werewolf stories. Between 1520 and 1630, when such tales circulated most widely, some 30,000 cases were recorded in France alone. Those investigating werewolves gradually came to believe that they suffered from a mental illness called lycanthropy. These patients, usually mentally disturbed or under the influence of hallucinogens, believed they could truly change their shape.
Because vampire bats have no need to chew their food, they have fewer teeth than other bats. Vampire bats help one another within a colony by regurgitating blood for bats that failed to find food. Vampire bats do not suck blood; they make a small cut in their prey and lap up the blood. There are also giant bats quite unlike ordinary vampire bats. An ordinary bat has a wingspan of about 30 centimeters, but the bats found in the Eslanian region are said to measure about a meter. Bats like dark places and are mostly active at night. Bats living in dark places such as dungeons act without regard to the time of day. One by one they are not very strong, but they sometimes attack in numbers, so be careful. In fact, many people find themselves struggling unexpectedly against such weak foes. Be careful too, because vampire bats living in Eslania have been known to take the form of vampires.
