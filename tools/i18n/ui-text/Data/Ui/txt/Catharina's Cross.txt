Only legendary accounts remain of the life of Catharina, a saint of the Catholic Church (feast day November 25). Of noble birth and well educated, at 18 she debated 50 pagan philosophers before the Emperor Maxentius and converted them. She also urged the empress to become a Christian, for which she was sentenced to the wheel; when the wheel shattered at her prayer, she was beheaded and died a martyr, so it is told. Her veneration began early in the Eastern Church, and from the 10th century she was widely venerated in the Roman Church as well. The cross she always carried is said to hold her holy spirit, and many seek it to protect themselves and to further their training.
