Quite apart from ordinary pleochroism, the defining trait of alexandrite is that it is a variety of chrysoberyl. The way its color changes with the light adds depth to its charm. Of all the chrysoberyl gems, alexandrite is the most expensive. Its rarity lies in its color change: dark green in natural daylight, red under lamplight at night. Named after an emperor, alexandrite is extremely scarce and seldom appears even in the jewelers of America and Europe. It was discovered in 1831 at an emerald mine in the Ural Mountains of Russia. Its main source is still the Urals where it was found, though Sri Lanka also produces much fine material, and being so rare, it is a gem that proclaims the wealth and honor of its owner.
