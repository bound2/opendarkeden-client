Swords were once made by only a handful of craftsmen, but as demand grew and the need for mass production became clear, many military suppliers took up sword-making. Among them, the S&W prototype that was standardized and released is the SWMS 3000, commonly known as the 'Niddle Edger'.
Made of a carbon steel alloy with a carbon content of 1.6% or more, it has excellent strength and hardness, and its blade curves much like a gladius, making it well suited to both cutting and thrusting. Early models had leather-wrapped grips, but rising maintenance costs led to synthetic resin instead, and some complain that the grip suffers for it.
