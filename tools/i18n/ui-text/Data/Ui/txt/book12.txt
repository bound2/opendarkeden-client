Records of vampires can be found even in parts of the Bible. Though those passages have since been removed from today's Bible, how the Babylonian Lilith was portrayed in still earlier ages can be traced in Hebrew apocryphal legend. Lilith, Adam's first wife before Eve was created, is the very prototype of the vampire. Angered by Adam's clumsiness in matters of love, Lilith left his side and became the queen of demons. Like the Lamia and the Striges, she too sucked the blood of little children and robbed sleeping young men of their potency. By Hebrew belief, Lilith flatly defied the law of Moses, which forbade drinking the blood of any living creature. "I say to the people of Israel: you shall not eat the blood of any creature, for the life of every creature is its blood. Whoever eats it shall be cut off from his people." (Leviticus 17:14) The Hebrews always had a complicated relationship with blood. To them blood was both the symbol of life and a mark of impurity. Blood was the sacred thing that breathed life into the body, and God alone ruled over life and death. At the same time it was bound up with the curse that had tainted humanity ever since Eve gave in to the Devil's temptation. Through that curse mankind fell, and menstruation, the punishment God laid upon the daughters of Eve, was taken as the proof of an impure wound. In Hebrew legend, menstrual blood is described as the source of all manner of disasters.
