A ring with an ankh engraved on its face. The ankh is the cross used in Egypt; it differs slightly from the Catholic cross in that the head of the cross is rounded. The ankh signifies eternal life; its vertical axis is the valley, its horizontal axis the desert, the lower half of the circle at the top the delta, and the upper half of that circle the Mediterranean Sea, and today's cross is said to derive from the ankh.
The ankh also stands for the universe; for all living things, both human and divine; for the key of knowledge that reveals mysteries and hidden wisdom; and for power, authority and oaths. Its form joins the male symbol of the god Osiris with the female symbol of the goddess Isis, representing the union of the two generative principles, heaven and earth. The ankh also symbolizes immortality, the afterlife and the future. One theory holds that the ankh takes the form of the 'Tree of Life', its oval standing for eternity and its cross for expansion in every direction, that is, the infinity of time and space.
