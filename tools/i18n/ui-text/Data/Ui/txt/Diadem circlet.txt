The circlet of Aton, worshipped in ancient Egypt as the sun god and the divine ruler of the world. This symbol does not appear as often as the ankh or the scarab, but it is no less representative of Egypt. It represents the god Aton of Egyptian myth. For reasons that remain unclear, the pharaoh Akhenaten banned the worship of every other Egyptian god and ordered that Aton alone be worshipped; Egyptian religion, in other words, became monotheistic. Aton was then depicted as ruler of the sun, ruler of the heavens and creator of the world, and came to symbolise divinity, perfection and power: in short, the symbol of the greatest authority. But under the boy king Tutankhamun, who took the throne a few years after Akhenaten's death, the worship of Aton declined, and Akhetaten, the city at the heart of that faith, fell into ruin.
